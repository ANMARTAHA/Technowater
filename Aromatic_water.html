Overview

Not the full lecture — just the definitions, rules and numbers you need to know cold.

What is an aromatic water?

  • BP definition: a clear, saturated aqueous solution of a volatile oil or other aromatic/volatile substance.
  • The dissolved substance has very low water solubility.
  • Aromatic material concentration is rather small — this is why aromatic waters are not therapeutically potent.
  • Main roles: flavored vehicle for water-soluble drugs, and the liquid phase of emulsions/suspensions.

5 official oral vehicles

  • Cinnamon water — flavored vehicle, pain-relief ointment
  • Orange water — flavored vehicle
  • Peppermint water — carminative, 15 ml dose
  • Camphor water — eye drops, rubefacient, cough
  • Chloroform water — expectorant, 5–15 ml dose

Non-oral aromatic waters

  • Rose water — perfume only
  • Hamamelis (witch hazel) water — rub, astringent, aftershave/cosmetic perfume
  • Hamamelis water can only be made by distillation

4 preparation methods

  • 1 — Distillation
  • 2 — Solution
  • 3 — Alternate solution (talc/silica)
  • 4 — Dilution

Stability — 4 risks

  • Mold growth — no preservative added
  • Loss of aroma on heat/light exposure
  • Cloudiness on cooling (saturated solution)
  • Salting out with electrolyte drugs
  • Oxidation → phosgene (chloroform water) / benzoic acid (bitter almond water)

Numbers worth memorizing

2 ml or 2 g / liter — excess oil, Solution method 15 min agitation 12 h standing time 15 g purified talc 1 liter water / 10 min — Alternate solution 50–55% alcohol — Dilution method 2 ml → 100 ml dilution < 1.5% final alcohol Peppermint dose: 15 ml Chloroform dose: 5–15 ml
